About

I'm a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Kennesaw State University and a Graduate Research Assistant in the ASTAI Lab, advised by Dr. Kazi Aminul Islam. My research is in AI security: physically realizable backdoor attacks and clean-label poisoning against remote-sensing models, the vulnerability of large language models, and defenses that identify and purify adversarial inputs. My first-author paper on robust physically realizable backdoor attacks appeared at IEEE TPS 2025, and our work on adversarial patch purification with spectral and diffusion models is under review at AAAI 2027.

Before the Ph.D., I completed a master's degree in Computer Science (AI concentration) at KSU, where I worked in the HIX Lab on real-time cybersickness detection and mitigation for standalone VR headsets—work supported by the U.S. Army Research Laboratory and published at IEEE VR 2024 and 2025. Earlier, I built production computer-vision systems as a Machine Learning Engineer at AI Samurai in Japan, and I earned my B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering from Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology, Bangladesh.

I also review for IEEE venues, including the IEEE Internet of Things Journal, and I'm open to collaborations on adversarial robustness, model security, and trustworthy AI.
